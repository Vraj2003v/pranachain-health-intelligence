# The three approaches: one method, three health areas

PranaPulse reads a person's own readings and shows where something has moved away from their usual pattern.
It never diagnoses, gives doses, or scores risk. The same four steps apply to all three health areas; what changes
is which readings are used and how far back we look.

## The four steps (same for every area)
1. **Set the person's own baseline.** Compare each person only with their own earlier readings, never with other people.
2. **Compare the latest readings with that baseline.** A change is reported only when it is larger than the person's usual day-to-day variation.
3. **Say how much data stands behind it.** Each finding carries a data-sufficiency flag: Sufficient, Limited, or held back if too few days have readings.
4. **Check the wording.** Every sentence passes a wording check (no diagnosis, no dose, no risk score) and shows its evidence: the dates compared, the averages and the days with data.

## The three areas

| | Cardiovascular | Metabolic | Women's health |
|---|---|---|---|
| What we read | Systolic and diastolic blood pressure, resting heart rate, HRV, steps; LDL cholesterol result | Weight, steps, sleep; HbA1c and fasting glucose results | Cycle start dates and cycle lengths |
| Past data used (baseline) | The 90 days before the latest 14 days | The 90 days before the latest 14 days | The recorded cycles (at least 4; "Sufficient" from 8) |
| Why that window | The partner advised that about 3 months is enough, especially for the heart | Weight and activity drift slowly, and HbA1c reflects about 3 months | Cycle length only makes sense over several cycles, not days |
| When it is reported | The latest 14 days differ from the baseline by more than 1.5 times the person's usual daily variation (placeholder), or 2 or more related measures move the same way over the year | Same rule; lab results count when HbA1c rises by 0.3 or more or fasting glucose by 10 or more | Cycle length varies by 4 or more days from cycle to cycle, or the average is under 21 or over 35 days |
| What the person sees | A card on the summary page and a section in Findings, with the evidence table | Same | Same |
| Limits | Few days with readings gives "Limited" or no finding | Same; lab results are few (every few months) | Needs at least 4 cycles; no daily readings |

## Open decisions (for the team to settle before Mon 2 Nov)
- **The 1.5x threshold is a placeholder.** On 900 workbooks the recent check flags 0 patients at 1.5x, 2 at 1.0x and 28 at 0.8x. The threshold decides how many people are flagged.
- **Longer baseline after a history event.** The partner said a year of data is only appropriate when the history shows an event such as surgery. This is not built yet. Proposal: if the history sheet lists such an event, start the baseline after it.
- **Women's health window.** The cycle check currently uses every cycle recorded. Proposal: use the most recent 6 cycles, so old cycles do not hide a recent change.

## One-sentence summary for the pitch
For every health area we compare a person with their own recent past, say how much data supports the finding, and show the evidence so a healthcare provider can check it.
